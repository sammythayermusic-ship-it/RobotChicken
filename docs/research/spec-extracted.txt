Overview
COCKFIGHT is a browser-based 1v1 3D fighting game where robotic chickens brawl, entry
requires holding $30 of $COCK, and every win pays instant SOL from the coin's 3%
trading fees, and the daily top 10 split a leaderboard pool. Nothing is wagered: the loser
keeps every token. The pitch in one line: hold to play, win to earn.
This document is the spec for a Claude Code session to build the whole thing: game,
server, site, chain layer, payouts. Read it top to bottom before writing code; the locked
decisions and build phases are the contract.
Done means: a stranger with $30 of $COCK can open cockfight.fun on a laptop or phone,
paste a wallet, get matched with another real human within 30 seconds, play a best-of-3
that feels like a real fighter (hit-stop, shake, sound), and see SOL land in their wallet within
10 seconds of winning, with the live fee pool and leaderboard on the front page.

Game design
A 2.5D fighter: 3D models and camera, but movement is locked to one axis (left/right) like
Street Fighter, which keeps netcode, hitboxes and readability simple. Camera is a fixed
side view with subtle dolly-in on hits and pull-back on distance.

Rounds and win conditions
Best of 3 rounds, 60 s each, 3 s pre-round countdown, 2 s KO freeze.
100 HP per chicken. Round ends on 0 HP , ring-out (leaving the arena edge), or timeout
(higher HP wins; equal HP = sudden death, first hit wins).
Heat meter: 0–100, +8 per hit landed, +3 per hit blocked, decays 1/s. Full Heat enables
the super. Heat resets each round.

COCKFIGHT — Build Plan
Each client predicts its own chicken from local inputs and interpolates the opponent 2
snapshots (100 ms) behind, so the opponent is smooth and your own inputs feel instant.
Protocol
Input message: { t: number, b: uint8 } where b is a bitmask (L, R, U, D, Space).
Sent every tick a button state changes, plus a keepalive every 10 ticks. Client tags with
its predicted tick.
Server keeps a 6-tick input buffer per player; a missing input repeats the last one.
Inputs older than 30 ticks are dropped and counted toward a jitter score.
Snapshot: Colyseus schema with tick, both fighters (pos, vel, hp, heat, state enum,
stateTick, facing), round timer, round wins, lastAck per player. Delta-encoded by
Colyseus.
Reconciliation: on snapshot, the client rewinds its sim to lastAck, re-applies buffered
local inputs, and blends the visual position over 3 frames if the correction is < 0.5 units;
snaps if larger.
Match lifecycle (state machine)
waiting → countdown(3 s) → fighting → roundEnd(2 s) → (countdown | matchEnd)
with matchEnd writing the result. Disconnect during fighting pauses the room 10 s;
reconnect via Colyseus allowReconnection resumes; timeout forfeits the match to the
connected player.
Latency handling
Region chosen by RTT probe; matches only pair players within 120 ms RTT of the same
region when queue depth allows, widening to 200 ms after 20 s in queue.
Server adds 2 ticks of input delay for both players to absorb jitter, shown nowhere in
the UI.
Per-match netQuality score stored with the result; matches with > 15% dropped
inputs on one side pay out at half rate (see Economics) to blunt lag-switching.
Replays
Every match stores its input log (two arrays of {t,b}, a few KB). Because the sim is
deterministic, any match can be replayed client-side from the log: used for the share page,
abuse review, and regression tests.

2. request.

Economics
Every trade of $COCK pays a 3% fee; the project claims it, keeps 30%, and 70% goes to
players as instant win payouts and a daily leaderboard pool. Payouts are sized from what
was actually claimed in the last 24 h, so the game can never promise more than fees
deliver, and a rising market cap raises payouts automatically. The front page shows the
live numbers:
"next win pays 0.041 SOL" and "today's leaderboard pool: 12.4 SOL"
